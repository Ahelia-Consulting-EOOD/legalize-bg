# Consolidation precedents abroad: how national legal-information systems consolidate from a raw amendment stream

**Date:** 2026-09-05. **Author:** research agent (Claude Fable 5.1).
**Source log:** `2026-09-05-consolidation-precedents-sources.md`. Every load-bearing claim below cites an `extract#N` row there.
**Builds on:** `2026-06-22-I1-legalize-ecosystem.md` (no Legalize country self-consolidates), `2026-06-22-I2-lawvm.md` (LawVM replay-compiler model, Finland numbers), `2026-06-22-I4-official-consolidation.md` (Bulgaria has no official consolidated source; ДВ is an amendment stream). Those findings are not repeated here.

**Context in one paragraph.** Bulgaria's State Gazette (Държавен вестник, ДВ) publishes promulgated acts and amending acts, not consolidated texts. Consolidation is done by private vendors whose output diverges from the Gazette. legalize-bg is moving to a model that rebuilds acts from ДВ where the Gazette text exists online (HTML since about 2005, PDF scans 1989 to 2004, nothing before 1989), audits the older base text, and attaches an explicit per-act provenance grade. This document asks how foreign national systems that consolidate from a raw amendment stream handle (1) divergence between the gazette and consolidated texts and (2) heterogeneous source formats across eras, and what transfers.

## 0. TL;DR

No national system detects gazette-versus-consolidation divergence by comparing texts; all prevent it by turning each amending act into a typed effects record first, then applying it, and all publish a bounded currency claim ("up to date with all changes known to be in force on or before <date>") rather than a correctness claim [extract#17, extract#31, extract#66].
Uncertainty is always categorical, never a score: applied versus outstanding effect, underlined unincorporated amendment, "(md not incorp)", "textlich nachgewiesen, dokumentarisch noch nicht abschließend bearbeitet", A-fout versus other error [extract#31, extract#77, extract#81, extract#88, extract#60].
Legal status is a four-rung ladder climbed only by statute: informative (Italy, Spain, France, Netherlands, Austria, Germany, eCFR), official publication (UK), evidence with the original prevailing (Canada, Australia, U.S. Code prima facie), the law itself (Estonia, New Zealand, U.S. positive-law titles) [extract#47, extract#18, extract#84, extract#86].
Even state-fed consolidators (BOE, DILA) disclaim, name the gazette as tie-breaker, and admit transcription errors [extract#71, extract#75].
Corrections are two channels: gazette errata absorbed into the original and not counted as amendments; consolidator errors logged as data with when they entered and left, or reported under a statutory editorial power [extract#41, extract#72, extract#69, extract#80].
Versioning is at the provision, with validity intervals and a per-version list of gazette items; the act at a date is a query [extract#54, extract#55, extract#62].
Only legislation.gov.uk publishes typed effects with application status, and only since 2002; BWB and RIS publish per-provision source lists; BOE publishes relation graphs; Normattiva publishes none [extract#13, extract#15, extract#62, extract#56, extract#73].
Nobody rebuilds pre-digital history from the gazette; everyone declares a base edition and date and lists what is not carried; Normattiva versions scanned parts as marked images [extract#4, extract#20, extract#40].
Finland's state bought the copyright of its vendor consolidation in 2025 rather than rebuild it; LawVM has one confirmed correction (Estonia, April 2026) and labels the rest "candidate" [extract#92, extract#91].
For legalize-bg: effects table first, provision-level versions with ДВ provenance, a categorical grade per version, a declared base edition for pre-1989, a corrections ledger, and the disclaimer carried into every API response (section 4, Options A to K).

## 2. Per-system findings

### 2.1 legislation.gov.uk (United Kingdom, The National Archives)

legislation.gov.uk is the UK state legislation site, run by The National Archives, and it is the clearest precedent for legalize-bg because it consolidates from raw amending Acts with an in-house editorial team rather than receiving consolidated text from a drafter. "Revised" is defined as "the editorial process of incorporating amendments and carrying through other effects into legislation" [extract#1]. Responsibility sits with "the Legislation Editorial Team at The National Archives in London and the staff of the Northern Ireland Statutory Publications Office in Belfast" [extract#2]. The team has also opened the work to "external expert participants from the legal publishing industry, government departments and the devolved administrations" [extract#29].

**What they consolidate from and how.** When a new Act arrives, editors analyse it to identify every impact on other legislation; the extracted information is tabulated and published as "Changes to Legislation" before the text edits are made, and the same table then guides the editor [extract#23, extract#30]. The editorial system "can apply certain effects automatically, such as repeals, commencements and non-textual amendments" [extract#32], but textual substitutions are applied by editors. Declared timescales are three months from an amendment coming into force [extract#7], four to eight weeks for the analysis stage [extract#23], and two weeks for the effects lists to appear [extract#16].

**Divergence handling and uncertainty labelling.** The gap between "what the law says" and "what the site shows" is made explicit rather than hidden. Any revised item with unapplied effects gets a "Changes to Legislation" banner, and on opening a provision "the outstanding effects are listed at the top of the provision" [extract#3, extract#8]. When everything known is applied, the banner asserts a bounded claim: "Equality Act 2010 is up to date with all changes known to be in force on or before 03 September 2026" [extract#17]. Editorial doubt is carried in annotations rather than in the text: the stated principle is to present the text "without gloss or comment", give "authority for changes to the text", and use additional editorial information "only sparingly ... to draw attention to some difficulty in the reading of the text arising from the way in which it has been amended" [extract#21, extract#22]. X-notes exist "to point to uncertainties in the text of very old Acts" [extract#26]. Known defects in the inherited data are documented as such and fixed as encountered: pre-basedate annotations "may be assigned to the wrong annotation types" [extract#25], and misplaced Northern Ireland footnotes "will be corrected as they are identified in the course of revision work" [extract#27].

**Legal status.** The 2013 guide calls the revised legislation "the official revised version of the statute book for the UK in electronic form" [extract#18]. No statement was found that it has evidentiary force in court, and no disclaimer that it is unofficial (source log N8). It is therefore "official" in the sense of state-published, not in the Canadian sense of statutory evidence (section 2.8).

**Point-in-time model.** Any date can be placed in a URI: "/ukpga/1985/67/1997-06-01 would refer to the version of The Transport Act 1985 that was in effect on 1st June 1997" [extract#11]; "/prospective" yields the text "were all prospective sections and amendments in effect" [extract#12]. A prospective version is one "with no start date, created as a result of an amendment that has not yet come into force" [extract#5]. In the XML, each section carries a Status of Prospective, Repealed or Discarded at the requested date, and non-valid sections carry Match="false" [extract#34]. Version metadata is addressable per date [extract#35].

**Provenance and quality grading.** Provenance is per change, not per act: seven annotation types (F textual amendments, C modifications not altering text, E extent, I commencement, P subordinate legislation made, M marginal citations, X editorial) give the authority for every alteration [extract#24]. The only quality grade is binary and per effect: "'Applied' and 'requires applied' statuses" [extract#31], surfaced to machines in the <ukm:UnappliedEffects> element [extract#33] and to readers in the banner.

**Machine-readable effects data.** Yes, and it is the most complete found. Each effect records "Effect type, Affecting provisions, Affected provisions, Savings, In force dates, Commencement authority, Extent and territorial application" plus applied statuses and notes [extract#31]. The public table has columns "Changed Provision", "Type of effect", "Affecting Provision", "Applied?", "Note", with effect values such as "words substituted", "words omitted", "repealed", "inserted", "coming into force", and exports as Atom and CSV [extract#13, extract#14]. The limit: "Changes made by legislation enacted prior to 2002 have already been incorporated into the content of the primary legislation and are not available as searchable lists" [extract#15]. One effect type deserves note for Bulgarian ЗИД practice: a "blanket amendment" is one "framed in such a way as to affect legislation generally rather than any specific enactment" (for example, substitute a name "wherever they occur in any enactment"), recorded as an attribute on the amending provision because its targets cannot be enumerated [extract#28].

**Pre-digital eras.** The base text is a frozen editorial edition, not a gazette rebuild. The base date is 1 February 1991; the text "was derived mainly from 'Statutes in Force' (SIF)", whose final revision incorporated everything up to that date [extract#4], and SIF "in turn derived from a series of earlier official revised editions, principally The Statutes Revised" [extract#19]. Consequences are stated plainly: the earliest revised version of a pre-1991 Act is "the revised text as it stood at 1 February 1991" [extract#36]; Acts "wholly repealed before the basedate" have no revised version [extract#20, extract#9]; and "Most secondary legislation is not currently revised and is held only in the form in which they were originally made" [extract#10], with pre-2018 instruments being brought up to date selectively [extract#6].

### 2.2 Normattiva (Italy)

Normattiva is the Italian state portal of legislation in force, produced and fed by the Istituto Poligrafico e Zecca dello Stato (IPZS, the state printer that also publishes the Gazzetta Ufficiale) for the Presidency of the Council of Ministers. Its organising concept is "multivigenza": "la ricostruzione del ciclo di vita di un atto normativo con le modifiche esplicite che esso ha subito nel tempo" (the reconstruction of an act's life cycle through the explicit modifications it has undergone) [extract#37]. The head of the Government's legislative office told the Chamber of Deputies in 2019 that the portal "contiene tutta la legislazione, primaria e secondaria, a partire dall'unità d'Italia in multivigenza" [extract#45], and the guide confirms coverage "dal 1861 ad oggi" [extract#40].

**Editorial or automatic.** Editorial. Updates are "frutto di un'attenta attività redazionale" and are guaranteed "di norma, entro tre giorni dalla data di pubblicazione dell'atto aggiornante" [extract#39]. Quality control is a sampling audit tied to the contractor's invoicing: "trimestralmente ... il Comitato compie un esame a campione dell'aggiornamento" [extract#46]. This is a governance model, not a technical one: the state buys consolidation from its printer and audits by sample.

**Legal status and divergence handling.** Normattiva takes the pure-disclaimer position. "Naturalmente Normattiva non ha valore legale, è sempre la Gazzetta Ufficiale che fa fede" [extract#44]; the footer text reads "I testi presenti nella banca dati 'Normattiva' non hanno carattere di ufficialità. L'unico testo ufficiale e definitivo è quello pubblicato sulla Gazzetta Ufficiale Italiana a mezzo stampa, che prevale in casi di discordanza" [extract#47]. No banner of outstanding changes and no per-act uncertainty label was found; the three-day update guarantee stands in for one [extract#39]. Corrections are handled by a rule rather than a flag: errata published before an act's entry into force "sono apportate direttamente sul testo originario dello stesso" [extract#41], so the "original" version shown is already corrected.

**Point-in-time model.** Three modes: the text "originariamente pubblicato in Gazzetta Ufficiale", the text "in vigore alla data di consultazione", and "vigente al ..." for a user-chosen date [extract#38, extract#50]. The identifier scheme embeds the version: a URN:NIR may end in "@originale" or "!vig=yyyy-mm-dd" [extract#48]. The open-data API returns per-article validity ranges ("articoloDataInizioVigenza": "20020101", "articoloDataFineVigenza": "99999999") and accepts a "dataVigenza" filter [extract#49].

**Provenance and grading.** Amended text is marked typographically, "in neretto all'interno di doppie parentesi (( ))" [extract#42], and each article shows update markers "agg.1", "agg.2", "orig." that open the amendment history [extract#50]. The history itself is a prose block, "AGGIORNAMENTO (6)", naming the amending act [extract#49]. There is no typed effects table and no quality grade per act or version (source log N10). The API does distinguish acts "senza aggiornamenti" from amended ones [extract#49].

**Heterogeneous formats and pre-digital eras.** Normattiva's answer to old acts is to keep the graphic part as an image and version it anyway: "Quando la parte testuale degli atti è associata a parti in formato grafico PDF, la 'multivigenza', opportunamente segnalata, si applica anche a tale formato" [extract#40]. This is the only system found that explicitly versions non-text (scanned) components inside the point-in-time model, with a visible marker that the component is graphic. Exports exist in "XML, PDF, EPUB e RTF" and "formato XML Akoma ntoso", and bulk access runs through the open-data portal dati.normattiva.it [extract#43].

### 2.3 RIS (Austria, Rechtsinformationssystem des Bundes)

RIS is the Austrian Federal Chancellery's legal information system. It is unusual in hosting both the authentic gazette and the unofficial consolidation in one place: since 1 January 2004 "die hier kundgemachte elektronische Fassung die authentische" is the legally binding Bundesgesetzblatt (BGBl) [extract#54], and "the legally binding version of the federal law gazette is published only in the RIS" [extract#52]. The same Chancellery unit produces "das rechtlich verbindliche Bundesgesetzblatt" and the "konsolidierten Texte ... für die Datenbank 'Bundesrecht konsolidiert'" [extract#53]. So the gazette publisher and the consolidator are one office, which removes the cross-organisation divergence problem Bulgaria has, but not the text-level one.

**Legal status and uncertainty labelling.** The consolidated database is described by the Austrian Parliament as "nicht immer tagesaktuell und auch nicht rechtlich verbindlich" [extract#51] and by N-Lex as "for information purposes only" with "no legal force" [extract#52]. Per-document free-text fields carry editorial caveats: "Beachte" ("Beachte für die konkrete Rechtsvorschrift") and "Anmerkung" [extract#56]. No list of outstanding, not-yet-incorporated amendments comparable to the UK banner could be verified, because the RIS user handbook and norm pages were unreachable throughout the session (source log N1, N14).

**Point-in-time model.** RIS versions at the smallest structural unit: "Dokumentationseinheit (ein Dokument) ist ein Paragraf oder ein Artikel oder eine Anlage einer Rechtsvorschrift" [extract#54]. Each such document has an entry-into-force and an end-of-force date, and the query surface is built on them: "FassungVom (YYYY-mm-dd)" returns the text in force on a date, or ranges over "VonInkrafttretensdatum", "BisInkrafttretensdatum", "VonAusserkrafttretensdatum", "BisAusserkrafttretensdatum" [extract#55]. "FassungVom" is defined as "Angabe jenes Datums, zu dem sich die Rechtsvorschrift in Kraft befinden soll" [extract#56]. A consolidated act at a date is therefore the set of paragraph-documents whose validity interval contains that date, not a stored whole-act snapshot.

**Provenance per version.** Each paragraph-document lists its amending gazette issues in "Aenderung": "Angabe der BGBl-Nummern, welche die konkrete Rechtsnorm novelliert haben" [extract#56]. The structural kind is typed ("ArtikelParagraphAnlage ... z.B. § 5, Art. 10, Anl. 2") and the query can restrict to "Artikel", "Paragraph" or "Anlage" [extract#55, extract#56]. The act-level number is explicitly labelled unofficial: "Gesetzesnummer" is the "nichtamtlichen Gesetzesnummer einer Rechtsnorm" [extract#56]. This is a per-version provenance list, but it is a list of sources, not typed operations; no effects table with operation types was found.

**Machine-readable access.** The open-data API queries "Bundesrecht" with "Applikation = BrKons" and supports "ImRisSeit" (documents new or changed in RIS within a window) [extract#55], which gives downstream users a change feed at the paragraph level.

**Pre-digital eras.** The authentic gazette starts in 2004; the BGBl for "1945 to 2003" is available in non-binding form, and historical gazettes "1780 to 1940" are linked [extract#52]. The consolidated database is anchored to its historical promulgation organs: the permitted "Kundmachungsorgan" values include "RGBl. Nr.", "StGBl. Nr.", "dRGBl. I S", "JGS Nr." and provincial gazettes [extract#55], meaning a consolidated paragraph can name a 19th-century gazette as its origin while its current text carries modern BGBl amendments in "Aenderung". Whether RIS exposes historical versions back to those origins, or only from a later base date, was not verified (source log N1).

### 2.4 Finlex and LawVM (Finland), additions only

Finlex is the Finnish Ministry of Justice's free legal database, maintained by the Legal Register Centre [extract#93]. The I2 study already established that its "up-to-date legislation" is a vendor-compiled, non-authoritative oracle and that LawVM replays the gazette (Säädöskokoelma) against it. Three things are new.

**The state bought the consolidation rights.** The renewed Finlex opened on 27 February 2025 with "entistä laajemmat ja ajantasaisemmat säädösaineistot", and a stated objective of the renewal was "ajantasaisen lainsäädännön sekä säädösmuutostietojen tekijänoikeuksien hankkiminen valtiolle pysyvästi" (acquiring for the state, permanently, the copyright in the up-to-date legislation and the amendment data) [extract#92]. Content production still sits with Edita Publishing Oy according to Government releases [extract#96, search snippet]. This is the Bulgarian problem in mirror image: Finland had vendor-owned consolidation inside a state portal and chose to buy the rights rather than rebuild. The Finlex pages read do not state whether the updated texts are official; the only status sentence is "The Ministry of Justice and the Legal Register Centre are not responsible for any content other than their own" [extract#95] (source log N11).

**Open data now ships both layers daily.** Downloads are "Updated legislation (4.1 GB)" and "Statute Book of Finland (12.0 GB)", with "packages are updated with new documents once a day" [extract#94], all "in Akoma Ntoso XML format and in a folder structure based on it" [extract#93]. The amendment register is expressed as a bounded currency claim, "Latest amendments in updated legislation ... up to and including statute 763/2026" [extract#95], which is the same device as the UK banner: a statement of the last input processed, not a claim of correctness. The Statute Book is offered "as text and as PDF facsimiles" [extract#95], so the gazette layer carries both a text and an image surface for the same issue.

**LawVM's status and its labelling discipline.** The home page marks the project "Beta-stage / pre-1.0" and lists twelve jurisdiction frontends (Finland, Estonia, UK, New Zealand, Poland, Norway, Sweden, EU, US, Switzerland, Japan, South Korea) [extract#91]. The one development after the I2 study is the Estonian case: an omitted phrase in the Auditors Activities Act was corrected by Riigi Teataja, with a "Public correction notice: 27 April 2026", and the page insists "This is one confirmed case, not a general accuracy claim"; further "three New Zealand and two UK candidate findings" sit below the confirmation threshold [extract#91]. The transferable point is the vocabulary: a divergence is a "candidate finding" until the publisher confirms it, and only then a "confirmed correction". No public per-statute confidence score or effects dataset was found on the page (source log N9).

### 2.5 wetten.overheid.nl / BWB (Netherlands)

wetten.overheid.nl is the Dutch government's consolidated-legislation site, backed by the Basiswettenbestand (BWB, "basic statute file"), a versioned XML store of every consolidated regulation. Attribution of the consolidation work to KOOP, the government's official-publications centre, appears only in search snippets and not on the help pages read (source log N13). The collection holds "zo'n 45.000 regelingen met meer dan 100.000 toestanden" (about 45,000 regulations with more than 100,000 states) [extract#64].

**Legal status.** The consolidated texts "vormt geen bekendmaking in de zin van de Grondwet" (are not a promulgation in the constitutional sense); "Alleen publicatie in het Tractatenblad, het Staatsblad, de Staatscourant en andere vanwege de overheid verkrijgbaar gestelde publicatiebladen heeft een officieel karakter" [extract#57, extract#61]. This is the same non-official posture as Normattiva, but paired with published service levels and error accounting.

**Divergence handling as measured error classes.** The site publishes a processing target, "minimaal 95% van de besluiten binnen twee werkdagen na publicatie ... en alle besluiten binnen vijf werkdagen" [extract#58], and it classifies its own defects: "fouten die kunnen leiden tot juridisch onjuiste gevolgtrekkingen (bijvoorbeeld verkeerde bedragen, ontbrekende regelingen of artikelen), aangeduid als A-fouten" versus errors "die niet tot misinterpretatie kunnen leiden", reporting "In het jaar 2015 zijn 10 A-fouten gemeld en hersteld" [extract#60]. No other system studied publishes a count of its own consolidation errors by severity.

**Uncertainty labelling.** Two cases are handled by explicit devices rather than silence. Retroactive amendments (a provision that "op 1 januari 2003 in werking treedt, maar ... terugwerkt tot bijvoorbeeld 1 juni 2002") get a warning box, and announced-but-not-yet-in-force amendments are stored as separate texts, "teksten van bestaande regelgeving waarin bekendgemaakte toekomstige wijzigingen zijn verwerkt" [extract#61].

**Point-in-time model and per-version provenance.** Every consolidated version is a "toestand" (state), an XML file validated against toestand_2016-1.xsd, and the change history is a separate WTI file ("wettechnische informatie") that "bevat alle informatie die als wetstechnische informatie in wetten.nl wordt getoond", validated against wti_2016-1.xsd, with element identifiers per Juriconnect 1.3.1 [extract#63]. Per article, the WTI overview "toont alle wijzigingen vanaf 1 mei 2002" and marks with an orange bar "welke versie betrekking heeft op het artikel dat u hebt opgevraagd"; each row carries "Ontstaansbron" (the originating Staatsblad, Staatscourant or Tractatenblad item, hyperlinked), "Inwerkingtreding", "Betreft" (kind of change) and, where relevant, "Terugwerkende kracht" [extract#62]. Each regulation is shipped as states plus one WTI file plus a manifest [extract#64].

**Machine-readable effects data.** The WTI is, in effect, a per-article effects table keyed by source publication, entry-into-force date and change kind, and changes can be pulled through "een service ... gebaseerd op SRU" [extract#64]. It is less granular than the UK's typed effects (no "words substituted" level), but it is bound to the gazette item that caused each state, which is exactly the provenance link legalize-bg lacks.

**Pre-digital eras.** BWB is a base-date system like legislation.gov.uk, with a much later base: consolidated history exists for laws "vanaf 1 mei 2002", ministerial regulations "vanaf 1 mei 2003", treaties "vanaf 1 januari 2005", and Caribbean Netherlands regulations "vanaf 10 oktober 2010" [extract#59]. The WTI overview accordingly starts at 1 May 2002 [extract#62]; earlier amendment history is not reconstructed.

### 2.6 Federal Register, eCFR and the U.S. Code (United States)

The Federal Register is the US daily official gazette for agency rules; the Code of Federal Regulations (CFR) is its official annual codification, "the codification of the general and permanent rules published in the Federal Register" [extract#67]; the eCFR is the continuously updated consolidation produced by the Office of the Federal Register (OFR) and the Government Publishing Office [extract#65]. The US is the clearest statement of the "unofficial until codified" model and, in the eCFR API, of corrections as first-class data [extract#69].

**Legal status, stated in terms of legal notice.** "The e-CFR is an unofficial editorial compilation of CFR material and Federal Register amendments." The Administrative Committee of the Federal Register authorised it "as an informational resource pending ACFR action to grant the e-CFR official legal status", and "Until the ACFR grants it official status, the e-CFR editorial compilation does not provide legal notice to the public or judicial notice to the courts" [extract#65]. GPO repeats that it is "not an official legal edition of the CFR", while the annual edition "is the codification of the general and permanent rules published in the Federal Register" [extract#67]. The status is therefore not "wrong" versus "right" but "gives notice" versus "does not".

**Divergence handling and currency.** Currency is declared per page and bounded: "The OFR updates the material in the e-CFR on a daily basis. Generally, the e-CFR is current within two business days. The current update status is displayed at the top of all e-CFR web pages" [extract#66]. Users are told how to close the gap themselves: "verify their results against the official edition of the CFR, the Federal Register, and the List of CFR Sections Affected (LSA)" [extract#66], where the LSA lists rules "published in the Federal Register since the most recent revision date of a CFR title" [extract#68]. The official codification is rebuilt on a fixed quarterly cycle, "titles 1-16 are revised as of January 1; titles 17-27 are revised as of April 1; titles 28-41 are revised as of July 1; titles 42-50 are revised as of October 1" [extract#67].

**Point-in-time model and machine-readable data.** ecfr.gov itself blocked fetching (source log N2), so the API is documented here from a third-party connector reference. It exposes "a list of historical content versions for a specific CFR title" with fields "date", "amendment_date", "issue_date", "identifier", "name", "part", "type", "substantive", "removed", and "full XML content for a given title on a specified CFR snapshot date"; title-level metadata carries "latest_amended_on", "latest_issue_date", "up_to_date_as_of" [extract#69]. Two design points matter. Versions distinguish the date an amendment took effect from the date it was issued, and mark whether a change was "substantive". And corrections to the compilation are a separate resource: "a list of historical corrections to the CFR, including Federal Register citations" with "corrective_action", "error_corrected", "error_occurred", "fr_citation" [extract#69]. That is a published ledger of the consolidator's own mistakes, each tied to when the error entered, when it left, and the gazette item that fixed it.

**A statutory two-grade legal status: the U.S. Code.** For statutes rather than regulations, federal law itself grades the consolidated text per title. Under 1 U.S.C. 204(a) the Code "shall ... establish prima facie the laws of the United States", but "Whenever titles of such Code shall have been enacted into positive law the text thereof shall be legal evidence of the laws therein contained, in all the courts of the United States" [extract#70]. In other words, an editorially compiled title is rebuttable evidence and the underlying Statutes at Large control on conflict, whereas a title that Congress has re-enacted as a whole is itself the law. The Office of the Law Revision Counsel's own pages were unreachable (source log N3), so its classification tables and release points are not described here.

**Pre-digital eras.** Not addressed by the pages read: the eCFR model starts from the official annual CFR, which is itself a periodic republication, so the "base text" problem is solved by the existence of a complete official codification each year rather than by rebuilding from the gazette [extract#67].

### 2.7 Contrast systems: BOE Legislación Consolidada (Spain) and Légifrance (France)

Both are state agencies that publish the gazette and the consolidation from one house: the Agencia Estatal Boletín Oficial del Estado (BOE) in Spain and the Direction de l'information légale et administrative (DILA) in France [extract#73, extract#76]. They are the "easy case" the I1 study described, yet both disclaim their consolidations in the same terms as the hard-case systems, "sin valor oficial" and "sans valeur officielle", which is the point of the contrast [extract#71, extract#74].

**BOE.** Consolidation is defined as integrating "en un solo texto, sin valor oficial, las modificaciones, correcciones y derogaciones"; the texts have "carácter meramente informativo" and "carecen de valor jurídico" [extract#71]. Status labels are per act ("Texto consolidado", "Norma vigente", "Norma derogada") and per update ("Última actualización publicada el") [extract#71]. Corrections are folded into the base rather than versioned: "Las correcciones de errores y erratas han sido incorporadas al texto original. No se consideran modificaciones" [extract#72], and the version set is the latest text, "versiones intermedias" for each modification, and the original [extract#72]. The open data carries "el análisis jurídico de la norma (materias, notas, referencias anteriores y posteriores)", that is, the amendment graph as typed cross-references, and it obliges reusers to label every text "texto consolidado de carácter meramente informativo" and to cite its "fecha de actualización" [extract#73]. Coverage is selective by design, "las normas más relevantes del ordenamiento jurídico", reaching back to "finales del siglo XIX" [extract#73].

**Légifrance.** DILA defines consolidation as integrating "dans un acte unique, sans valeur officielle, les modifications et les corrections successives apportées à un texte" [extract#74], and, mechanically, as rewriting each code article when it is modified [extract#76]. It is the only official site found that admits the failure mode in writing: "la consolidation n'est pas à l'abri d'erreurs de transcription ; elle implique en tout état de cause un délai de mise à jour", so users may need "de vérifier le contenu du texte recherché en se reportant aux actes modificatifs, accessibles à partir du fonds « Journal officiel »" [extract#75]. Texts are exposed "dans leur version en vigueur et également dans leurs versions antérieures ou à venir" [extract#76], and DILA states that exhaustive coverage of national legislation "n'est pas encore totalement atteint à ce jour" [extract#76]. The LEGI per-article state vocabulary (in force, deferred, repealed, and so on) is widely described in third-party guides but was not verified from a DILA document (source log N12).

**What the contrast shows.** Even where the drafter, the gazette and the consolidator are the same institution, the consolidated text is labelled informative, the gazette is named as the tie-breaker, corrections are absorbed into the original rather than treated as amendments, and the amendment relations are published as data next to the text [extract#71, extract#73, extract#74, extract#75]. The Bulgarian situation differs in who does the work, not in what the output is allowed to claim.

### 2.8 Other systems with an explicit quality, provenance or legal-status grade

Six further systems publish an explicit status per compilation or per version. None publishes a numeric score (source log N9); all publish categorical grades backed by a rule, from Australia's "reliable source of information ... unless proven to the contrary" [extract#77] to Estonia's consolidated text with "term of validity" published as the official act [extract#86].

**Australia, Federal Register of Legislation (Office of Parliamentary Counsel).** "A compilation is a version of a law that shows the text of the law as amended at a particular point in time" [extract#77]. Its endnotes are the provenance record: "the amendment history lists the amendments that have been incorporated into the law" [extract#78]. Three uncertainty devices are formal. First, amendments not yet applied are visible in the text itself: "Any unincorporated amendments known at the compilation date will be underlined" [extract#77]. Second, drafting defects in the amending act get a name and a code: "A misdescribed amendment is an amendment that does not accurately describe how an amendment is to be made" [extract#77]; where it cannot be given effect "the amendment is not incorporated and (md not incorp) is added to the amendment history" [extract#81, search snippet]. Third, the consolidator's own fixes are a statutory power with a public ledger: an editorial change is made "by the First Parliamentary Counsel under section 15V of the Legislation Act 2003" [extract#78], limited to typographical, grammatical, numbering, cross-reference and similar errors [extract#79], never "to change the effect of the law, to resolve an ambiguity in the law or to rewrite legislation", and reported in published "Editorial changes reports" [extract#80]. Legal status is graded by format: an authorised version is "taken to be a reliable source of information by a court or tribunal unless proven to the contrary" [extract#77].

**New Zealand, legislation.govt.nz (Parliamentary Counsel Office).** The Legislation Act 2019 makes the electronic consolidation itself official: "The PCO may publish (1) official electronic versions of legislation; and (2) official printed versions of legislation" (s 78). Editorial correction is a delegated power, "The Chief Parliamentary Counsel may authorise the PCO to make changes referred to in section 87 to a version of the legislation" (s 86), and s 89 requires the PCO to "indicate that fact in a suitable place" and "outline in general terms ... the changes made" [extract#82, third-party mirror; primary hosts blocked, source log N5].

**Canada, Justice Laws Website.** "As of June 1, 2009, the consolidated Acts and regulations on the Justice Laws Website are 'official', meaning that they can be used for evidentiary purposes" [extract#83]. The statute both grants and bounds this: "Every copy of a consolidated statute or consolidated regulation published by the Minister ... is evidence of that statute or regulation and of its contents", but "In the event of an inconsistency between a consolidated statute ... and the original statute or a subsequent amendment as certified by the Clerk of the Parliaments ... the original statute or amendment prevails" [extract#84]. Operationally the site "is generally updated every two weeks", offers "Previous Versions" from "January 1, 2003 onwards for the Acts and March 22, 2006 onwards for the regulations", and lists "Amendments Not in Force" as provisions "not in force (eg. not yet in effect) at the current consolidation date" [extract#85].

**Estonia, Riigi Teataja.** The strongest legal grade found: "As of June 1, 2010, with coming into force of the new Riigi Teataja Act, the eRT became the only official publication", and consolidation is part of promulgation, "Each time when a legislative act is changed, a new consolidated text is drafted and published along with the information pertaining to the draft's term of validity"; consolidated laws exist from 1990 and ministerial regulations from 1 June 2002 [extract#86]. The FAQ states that consolidated texts "have legal power according to Subsection 1 of § 3 of the Riigi Teataja Act" [extract#87, search snippet; page is JavaScript-only, source log N6].

**Germany, Gesetze im Internet (Federal Ministry of Justice with juris).** Texts are "nicht amtliche Texte"; the authentic source is the Bundesgesetzblatt, "seit dem 1. Januar 2023 die amtliche Fassung des Bundesgesetzblatts in elektronischer Form ausgegeben". The "Stand:" line names "das Datum der Ausfertigung des Stammgesetzes bzw. des letzten Änderungsgesetzes"; provisions amended but not yet in force are replaced only "zu ihrem Inkrafttreten"; and a two-stage quality flag exists, "textlich nachgewiesen, dokumentarisch noch nicht abschließend bearbeitet" (text verified, documentary processing not yet complete), which the site explains concerns metadata only, not the text [extract#88].

**Ireland, Law Reform Commission Revised Acts.** "A Revised Act is an administrative consolidation of that Act" [extract#90], carrying a liability disclaimer: the Commission "can assume no responsibility for and give no guarantees, undertakings or warranties concerning the accuracy, completeness or up to date nature of the information provided" [extract#89, search snippet]. Included because it is a consolidation done by a body that is neither the drafter nor the gazette publisher, the closest institutional analogue to legalize-bg.

## 3. Cross-system synthesis

### 3.1 Question 1: divergence between gazette and consolidated text

**Detection is procedural, not comparative.** No state system studied detects divergence by diffing its consolidation against another consolidator's text. They prevent divergence by pipeline discipline (analyse the amending act, tabulate its effects, then apply them) and they declare the boundary of what has been processed. The UK banner says an Act "is up to date with all changes known to be in force on or before 03 September 2026" [extract#17]; the eCFR says it "is current within two business days" and shows the status "at the top of all e-CFR web pages" [extract#66]; Finlex says amendments are included "up to and including statute 763/2026" [extract#95]; Canada shows a "current to" date and updates "every two weeks" [extract#85]. The only cross-consolidation comparison found is external to the state: LawVM, which labels each mismatch a "candidate finding" until the publisher confirms it [extract#91].

**Uncertainty is labelled categorically at three levels.** Per effect: the UK's "'Applied' and 'requires applied' statuses" [extract#31], Australia's underlined "unincorporated amendments" [extract#77], Canada's "Amendments Not in Force" list [extract#85], and the Dutch practice of storing announced future amendments as separate texts [extract#61]. Per act or version: Germany's "Stand:" date plus the flag "textlich nachgewiesen, dokumentarisch noch nicht abschließend bearbeitet" [extract#88], Spain's "Última actualización publicada el" [extract#71], the eCFR's "up_to_date_as_of" per title [extract#69]. Per text location: UK X-notes "to point to uncertainties in the text of very old Acts" [extract#26], RIS "Beachte" and "Anmerkung" fields [extract#56], Dutch warning boxes for retroactive provisions [extract#61]. No system publishes a numeric confidence (source log N9).

**Legal status forms a four-step ladder.** Informative only, gazette prevails: Normattiva [extract#44, extract#47], BOE [extract#71], Légifrance [extract#74], BWB [extract#57], RIS [extract#51, extract#52], Gesetze im Internet [extract#88], eCFR [extract#65], Irish Revised Acts [extract#89]. Official state publication without an evidentiary claim: legislation.gov.uk [extract#18]. Evidence, but the original prevails on inconsistency: Canada [extract#84], Australia's authorised versions "unless proven to the contrary" [extract#77], the non-positive-law titles of the U.S. Code as "prima facie" law [extract#70]. The consolidation is itself the law: Estonia [extract#86, extract#87], New Zealand's official electronic versions [extract#82], positive-law titles of the U.S. Code [extract#70]. The higher rungs are reached by statute, never by technical quality alone.

**Corrections split into gazette-side and consolidator-side, and the systems treat them differently.** Gazette errata are absorbed into the original text and not counted as amendments: "le rettifiche ... sono apportate direttamente sul testo originario" [extract#41]; "Las correcciones de errores y erratas han sido incorporadas al texto original. No se consideran modificaciones" [extract#72]. Consolidator-side corrections are either a statutory editorial power with a public report (Australia's s 15V changes, limited to clerical and cross-reference errors and reported in "Editorial changes reports" [extract#78, extract#79, extract#80]; New Zealand's s 86 with a duty to "indicate that fact" and "outline in general terms" [extract#82]), or a data resource (the eCFR's corrections with "error_occurred", "error_corrected", "fr_citation" [extract#69]), or a measured error class ("A-fouten", "10 A-fouten gemeld en hersteld" in 2015 [extract#60]). Inherited defects are declared and fixed on encounter: "some annotations dating from before the basedate may be assigned to the wrong annotation types" [extract#25]; misplaced footnotes "will be corrected as they are identified" [extract#27]. Légifrance goes furthest in admitting the class of error: "la consolidation n'est pas à l'abri d'erreurs de transcription" [extract#75].

**Pre-digital eras are handled by a declared base edition, not by rebuilding.** Every system with history has a base date and an inherited base text: UK 1 February 1991 from Statutes in Force, itself descended from The Statutes Revised [extract#4, extract#19]; Netherlands 1 May 2002 [extract#59]; Canada 2003 for Acts and 2006 for regulations [extract#85]; Estonia 1990 for laws and 2002 for ministerial regulations [extract#86]; the US relies on the official annual CFR edition as the periodic base [extract#67]; Austria's authentic gazette starts in 2004 with 1945 to 2003 non-binding [extract#52]. What is not carried is stated: Acts "wholly repealed before the basedate", Acts superseded by 1991 consolidations, and most secondary legislation [extract#20, extract#9, extract#10]. Normattiva alone claims multivigenza back to 1861 and does so by versioning scanned parts as images, "opportunamente segnalata" [extract#40, extract#45]; how those 19th-century histories were constructed was not verified.

### 3.2 Question 2: heterogeneous formats, per-version provenance, point-in-time, effects data

**The unit of versioning is the provision, and the act-at-a-date is a query.** RIS stores one document per "Paragraf oder ein Artikel oder eine Anlage" with entry and exit dates and resolves "FassungVom" over them [extract#54, extract#55]. BWB stores article-level states and a per-article WTI history [extract#62, extract#63]. CLML marks each section with RestrictStartDate, RestrictEndDate and a Status at the requested date [extract#34]. Normattiva's API returns "articoloDataInizioVigenza" and "articoloDataFineVigenza" per article [extract#49]. The eCFR lists content versions per section with "amendment_date", "issue_date" and a "substantive" flag [extract#69]. This provision-interval model is what lets heterogeneous acts coexist: an old act with unnumbered paragraphs and a modern act with lettered articles are both sets of addressable units with validity intervals.

**Provenance per version is a list of gazette items, with dates and usually a change kind.** RIS "Aenderung" gives "die BGBl-Nummern, welche die konkrete Rechtsnorm novelliert haben" [extract#56]. BWB's WTI row gives "Ontstaansbron" hyperlinked to the Staatsblad item, "Inwerkingtreding" and "Betreft" [extract#62]. UK annotations give "authority for changes to the text" through F, C, E, I, P, M and X notes [extract#21, extract#24]. Australia's endnotes carry the "amendment history" of incorporated amendments [extract#78]. Normattiva's "AGGIORNAMENTO (n)" blocks name the amending act in prose [extract#49]. Estonia binds the consolidated text to the amending act at publication, "published along with the information pertaining to the draft's term of validity" [extract#86]. In every case the provenance anchor is the gazette item, never the consolidator's own edit.

**Point-in-time is exposed as a date in the identifier or a date parameter, with the future as a first-class state.** Dated URIs and "/prospective" in the UK [extract#11, extract#12]; "@originale" and "!vig=yyyy-mm-dd" suffixes on Italian URNs [extract#48]; "FassungVom" in Austria [extract#55]; a snapshot "date" for full XML in the eCFR [extract#69]; "Previous Versions" in Canada [extract#85]; "versions antérieures ou à venir" in France [extract#76]. Not-yet-in-force text is modelled explicitly: a UK prospective version has "no start date" [extract#5], the Dutch base holds texts "waarin bekendgemaakte toekomstige wijzigingen zijn verwerkt" [extract#61], Canada lists "Amendments Not in Force" [extract#85], and Gesetze im Internet swaps text only "zu ihrem Inkrafttreten" [extract#88].

**Quality and provenance grading is categorical everywhere.** The table gathers what each system exposes; none is a score, and the closest thing to a graded scale is the U.S. Code's statutory pair "prima facie" versus "legal evidence" [extract#70] and Germany's two-stage "textlich nachgewiesen, dokumentarisch noch nicht abschließend bearbeitet" [extract#88].

**Machine-readable effects data exists in three shapes.** Typed operations with application status: only the UK, whose effect records carry "Effect type, Affecting provisions, Affected provisions, Savings, In force dates, Commencement authority, Extent" and applied statuses [extract#31], exported as Atom and CSV [extract#13] and echoed in <ukm:UnappliedEffects> [extract#33], but only for changes since 2002 [extract#15]. Per-provision change records keyed by source: BWB's WTI [extract#62, extract#63] and RIS "Aenderung" [extract#56]. Act-level relation graphs: BOE's "referencias anteriores y posteriores" [extract#73]. Normattiva publishes none [source log N10], and the eCFR publishes versions and corrections rather than operations [extract#69].

**Heterogeneous formats are handled by declared exclusions and on-encounter repair, not by uniform parsing.** The UK excludes whole categories from revision and says so [extract#20, extract#10], inherits misclassified annotations and fixes them "as we come across them" [extract#25], and uses X-notes where "variations in editorial practice inherited from older versions" cause difficulty [extract#26]. Normattiva versions graphic PDF parts with a marker [extract#40]. Finlex offers the gazette both as text and as PDF facsimile [extract#95]. Australia names the defective-instruction case, "misdescribed amendment", and records non-incorporation rather than guessing [extract#77, extract#81]. Nobody claims to have parsed every era into one clean structure; they publish what they could not do.

## 4. What transfers to legalize-bg

Each option names its precedent and its limit. They are independent; the owner can adopt any subset. Bulgarian terms: ЗИД (закон за изменение и допълнение, amending act), поправка (corrigendum), ДВ (Държавен вестник, State Gazette).

**Option A: a bounded currency statement per act, in the frontmatter and in every MCP response.** State the last ДВ issue processed and the date, in the UK form "up to date with all changes known to be in force on or before <date>" [extract#17], the eCFR form "current within two business days" [extract#66], or the Finlex form "up to and including statute 763/2026" [extract#95]. Limit: this asserts what was processed, not that it was processed correctly. It replaces the current implicit claim of the lex.bg photograph with an explicit one.

**Option B: make the effects table the primary artefact and publish unapplied effects.** Follow legislation.gov.uk: analyse each ЗИД into typed effects (target provision, effect type, in-force date, source ДВ item, applied status) before touching text, and expose the outstanding ones in metadata as the UK does in <ukm:UnappliedEffects> [extract#30, extract#31, extract#33]. This also satisfies the I2 replay model, whose ops are the same records with a kernel to execute them. Limit: the UK only has this for changes since 2002 and folds earlier ones into the text [extract#15]; legalize-bg will likewise have typed effects only from the ДВ HTML era (about 2005) and prose or vendor-derived effects before it.

**Option C: version at the provision, with validity intervals and a per-version source list.** Adopt the RIS unit ("ein Paragraf oder ein Artikel oder eine Anlage") with entry and exit dates and resolve an act at a date as a query [extract#54, extract#55], and attach to each version the BWB-style row: originating ДВ item, entry-into-force date, change kind [extract#62]. Limit: requires stable provision identity across renumbering; the I2 study's lineage and address-chain design is the answer, and RIS itself labels its act-level number "nichtamtlich" [extract#56], a reminder that identifiers are the consolidator's, not the law's.

**Option D: a categorical provenance grade per version, modelled on statutory tiers, never a score.** Precedents are all categorical: positive law versus prima facie [extract#70], authorised version versus other [extract#77], "textlich nachgewiesen, dokumentarisch noch nicht abschließend bearbeitet" [extract#88], applied versus requires applied [extract#31]. A workable ladder for legalize-bg: rebuilt from ДВ HTML with replay verified against an oracle; rebuilt from ДВ PDF scan read by vision; vendor base text audited against ДВ for all post-2005 amendments; vendor base text unaudited. Limit: the grade belongs to a version, so it must be recomputed when a new ЗИД lands, and no system found publishes a numeric confidence to borrow [source log N9].

**Option E: a named category for defective amending instructions, with a non-incorporation marker.** Australia's "misdescribed amendment" and "(md not incorp)" [extract#77, extract#81] and the UK's X-notes for reading difficulties [extract#22, extract#26] give the vocabulary for a ЗИД that cites a non-existent алинея or replaces words that are not present. Record the effect, mark it not incorporated, keep the text unchanged. Limit: Australia and New Zealand may then fix the text under a statutory editorial power [extract#78, extract#82]; legalize-bg has no such power and must stop at the flag.

**Option F: a corrections ledger with two channels.** Gazette-side поправка are absorbed into the base and not counted as amendments, per Normattiva and BOE [extract#41, extract#72], but recorded with their ДВ citation. Consolidator-side corrections are stored as data with the eCFR fields "error_occurred", "error_corrected" and the fixing source [extract#69], classified by severity as BWB does ("A-fouten" that can mislead legally, versus others) and counted per period [extract#60]. Limit: the FR-020 commit history already gives when a text changed; the ledger adds why, and it needs an editorial judgement of severity that the pipeline cannot make alone.

**Option G: declare a base edition and date for the pre-1989 era, and list what is not carried.** The UK took Statutes in Force at 1 February 1991 as base and states that wholly repealed Acts, 1991 consolidations and most secondary legislation are not revised [extract#4, extract#20, extract#9, extract#10]; the Netherlands starts at 2002 [extract#59]; Canada at 2003 [extract#85]. For legalize-bg the base edition is the lex.bg photograph as of the bootstrap date, graded at the lowest tier of Option D, with a per-act statement of the earliest ДВ item verified. Limit: the UK still finds inherited defects thirty years later and repairs them on encounter [extract#25, extract#27]; a declared base does not make the inherited text right, it makes the uncertainty honest.

**Option H: version scanned components as images with a visible marker.** Normattiva applies multivigenza to graphic PDF parts "opportunamente segnalata" [extract#40]; Finlex ships gazette issues as text and PDF facsimile side by side [extract#95]. For приложения, тарифи and tables in the 1989 to 2004 scan era, store the page image as the version's content and grade it accordingly rather than force a lossy text transcription. Limit: image versions cannot be diffed or searched; the grade must say so.

**Option I: disclaimer plus tie-break rule, carried into reuse.** Every system, including the state-fed ones, says the consolidation has no official value and the gazette prevails [extract#47, extract#57, extract#71, extract#74]; Légifrance adds that transcription errors are possible and points users to the amending acts [extract#75]; BOE obliges reusers to carry the label and the update date [extract#73]. legalize-bg should put both in the frontmatter and in tools.json response metadata so downstream consumers cannot drop them. Limit: a disclaimer does not reduce error, it allocates responsibility; the FR-028 API must propagate it.

**Option J: candidate versus confirmed divergence vocabulary for oracle comparisons.** LawVM reports mismatches as "candidate findings" and upgrades one to a "confirmed" correction only after the publisher acts [extract#91]. Apply this to legalize-bg's lex.bg and Ministry of Justice oracle checks (I2 lanes, I4 second oracle): a byte divergence is a candidate until a ДВ source resolves it. Limit: confirmation needs a human reading of the ДВ item; the pipeline can only rank candidates.

**Option K: publish an editorial-changes report.** Australia publishes running reports of every s 15V editorial change [extract#80]; New Zealand must "indicate that fact" and "outline in general terms" [extract#82]. legalize-bg's parser normalisations (superscript indices such as чл. 5а¹, unnumbered paragraphs given synthetic addresses, quotation-mark normalisation) are editorial changes in this sense and should be listed per act in a running report. Limit: this is a discipline, not a mechanism; it costs nothing technically and everything in consistency.

## 5. Open questions and negative results

**No system publishes a numeric quality or confidence grade.** Across thirteen systems the grades are categorical and rule-backed (source log N9). A legalize-bg design that outputs a percentage per act would be without precedent; the precedent is tiers with definitions.

**No state system publishes a comparison of its consolidation against a third party's.** Divergence handling is procedural (effects tables, currency statements, error classes). The only comparative work found is LawVM, which is research software with one confirmed correction [extract#91]. legalize-bg's oracle comparison against lex.bg and the Ministry of Justice portal therefore has no state precedent to copy for its reporting format; Option J borrows LawVM's vocabulary instead.

**No system rebuilds pre-base history from gazette scans.** All adopt an inherited edition at a base date [extract#4, extract#59, extract#85, extract#86]. Normattiva's claim of multivigenza from 1861 [extract#45] is the one apparent exception, but whether 19th-century histories were reconstructed or merely digitised from IPZS's own gazette archive was not verified. legalize-bg's plan to rebuild the 1989 to 2004 era from PDF scans is therefore more ambitious than any precedent found.

**Unverified because the host was unreachable** (source log N1 to N7): how far back RIS historical versions reach and the exact wording of its disclaimer; the eCFR's earliest point-in-time date and its own API documentation; the U.S. Code classification tables and release points; the Australian compilation endnote boilerplate as printed; New Zealand's PCO editorial conventions; Riigi Teataja's digital-stamp and error-correction mechanics; Normattiva's home-page disclaimer as rendered. Each is cited only through a flagged fallback or omitted.

**Unverified from a primary document** (source log N11 to N13): the official or unofficial status of Finlex "Updated legislation" on the 2025 site; the LEGI per-article state vocabulary; the attribution of BWB consolidation to KOOP.

**Open question for the owner.** The legal-status ladder in section 3.1 is climbed by statute. Estonia, New Zealand and Canada made consolidations official by amending their gazette or legislation acts [extract#86, extract#82, extract#84]. Whether Bulgaria's Закон за нормативните актове could or should ever recognise a consolidated text is a policy question outside this study; until then legalize-bg sits on the bottom rung with Normattiva, BOE and Légifrance, and Option I is the honest label for that.

**Open question for the ДВ rebuild design.** The UK, Netherlands and Canada all chose the base date to coincide with the start of their machine-readable gazette or effects data [extract#15, extract#59, extract#85]. legalize-bg has two candidate base dates, 2005 (ДВ HTML) and 1989 (ДВ scans), and the precedents suggest treating them as two grade boundaries (Option D) rather than one base.
