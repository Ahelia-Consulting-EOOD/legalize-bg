How an act reaches the corpus: as built, and as planned next

Fifty acts have come all the way from their lex.bg page to a commit in git, through the reading path and the single write gate. That path copies lex.bg's text character for character and judges only formatting, so an act that lex.bg holds wrong reaches git looking right. The Закон за обществения транспорт did exactly that: it was committed again with its additional provision, § 1 and its twelve definitions, missing.

Section 1 shows the way of work planned next, agreed with the owner on 22 October 2026. It has two tasks. The first defines a starting state of sufficient quality: every act read from lex.bg, except the acts on a kept list of exceptions, which are investigated and fixed from the source the list names. Being on the list is not an outcome: every listed act has a route that ends with the act committed correct in git, and only such acts count as done. The second task rebuilds that starting state forward with each new issue of the State Gazette (Държавен вестник), and compares it with lex.bg from time to time to measure how good the consolidation is. Sections 2 to 5 show the path as built. Section 6 proposes the steps for both tasks.

1. The planned way of work: a starting state, rebuilt forward

Nobody seems to consolidate the whole history of every act by hand, and it is not known yet whether a machine can either. So the work does not rebuild each act from its first Gazette issue. It starts from the acts as they stand and builds on them, in two tasks.

Task 1: a starting state of sufficient quality

Two things change in the path. Before an act is read, its source is decided: every act not on the exceptions list is read from lex.bg as today. An act on the list is investigated first, to name its defect and the source that holds it right, and its source route then fetches that text. After the reading, a content check finds any act that has lost text and sends it to the list and into its route, instead of writing it. The witnesses show where the text differs from lex.bg and from the Ministry of Justice portal, and each difference is settled and recorded. Every branch ends in the same place: the correct act, committed through the gate. The register of act profiles and the inventory of lex.bg's defects bring more acts to the list and order the audit.

Every route is bounded. It runs only as far as it knows what to do, and stops at a fixed point: when its known steps are used up, when a step changes nothing, or when the next step is not defined. Where it stops without a correct act, it writes a case file for that act: the exception, what was tried, the lessons learnt and a proposal. The owner reads the case files of a run together, at the run's end, and the decision becomes a ruling, a rule or a new route; then the act goes through its route again. No loop runs on toward the goal past that point.

The routes may become a project skill for updating this repository, so that any session can run them the same way. When the work is published, the same routes are developed into a harness.

Task 2: rebuilt with each Gazette issue, and tested against lex.bg

From the starting state on, the corpus follows the Gazette. Each new issue is fetched; its materials are attributed to the acts they change, and their instructions are turned into changes to those acts and applied. The result goes through the same checks and the same write gate, and is committed as the act after that issue. Most of what the Gazette publishes is amendments, so this needs the engine of plan step 5; a new act is rare, and is simply read whole. From time to time the corpus is compared with lex.bg and with the Ministry of Justice portal. They are witnesses, not truth: each difference is settled against the Gazette, and the count of differences that were the engine's errors is the measure of the consolidation's quality.

The engine is bounded like every route. Where it cannot apply a material with certainty, it stops for that act with a case file, and the owner's ruling decides what follows, for example re-reading that act from lex.bg once lex.bg has caught up. Whether the engine can be built at all is tested first on a few representative acts of each kind of act: their history is rebuilt from the Gazette and compared with the witnesses. Once it has passed that test, it rebuilds an act's complete history only case by case, where one is needed, such as a difference to settle or a gap in a lex.bg fetch; nobody rebuilds every act's history since 2003.

2. The path as built

3. The ladder and the residue

The checks turn every failure into an exception on one block. The ladder gives each excepted block two more tries: its block goes back to the labeller, with the block before and the block after it. Whatever is still excepted after round 2 is the residue, and it is sorted by what another round could change.

4. The write path, one act at a time

write_staged(slug, run_dir) trusts nothing it finds in the run folder. It reads the page again, splits it again, assembles the body again and checks it again, and only then builds the frontmatter and calls the gate. A failure at any step stops that act and touches nothing else.

5. What a written act leaves in git

The run record (D-073) goes into the same commit as the act, so anyone can rebuild the body from the page and check that the result matches. Files that can be rebuilt stay out of git.

6. Proposed steps to build it

The steps follow the two tasks. Task 1's steps come from the agreed record of 22 October (docs/plans/2026-10-22-owner-intent-sources-and-act-profiles.md): the exceptions list first, with the bounds that every route needs. S2 and S3 can run side by side after S1; S4 needs S3's list of acts begun before the archive, and S5 needs S3's profiles. Task 2's steps are plan steps 5 and 8 as decision D-074 narrowed them, and a periodic test that is my addition to give the owner's quality measure a fixed form. Each step ends with acts committed correct in git, or says why it commits none.

Task 1: the starting state

S0 · with S1

Bounds and case files, for every route

  • A route stops at its known end: the ladder after round 2, an investigation after one run (a second only on new evidence), a source route after one fetch per source.
  • It stops earlier when a step changes nothing (the same answer twice, as the ladder's rule_needed already does) or when the next step is not defined (no route yet for this defect or this era).
  • Never: a third round, the same step again on the same inputs, an edit made so that a check passes.
  • On a stop, a case file per act in git: the exception, what was tried with its evidence, the lessons learnt, the proposal. Its form is fixed, so a check refuses a stopped act without one.
  • The owner reads a run's case files together at its end, not one act at a time during the run; no run goes on past its bound.

Done when the bounds are in the route driver with tests, and the case-file form is checked. It commits no act itself.

S1 · first

The exceptions list and its routes

  • A data file, docs/data/source-exceptions.yaml: per act its slug, its defect, the source and the source's address, the route that commits it, the evidence (a decision or audit) and where it stands (listed, investigated, committed).
  • Mechanical checks, because the form is fixed: the file loads against its schema; every entry names a route; the write path refuses a listed act on the lex.bg path; a report prints each entry's distance to its commit.
  • The investigation: a lean project agent reads what the scripts fetched for a listed act and records its defect, its source and its route as data.
  • The content check: an empty section or other lost text sends the act to the list and into its route, instead of writing it. This changes D-072 ruling 7 for that finding.
  • The witnesses: the text read is compared with lex.bg and with the Ministry of Justice portal, and every difference is settled and recorded.
  • First entries, fixed as ordinary entries with no specification of their own. The transport law first, because the owner uses it daily: lex.bg drops its § 1, and the act was never amended, so its Gazette text, ДВ бр. 32/2026, is the whole act; a text search of the materials since then makes sure. The Закон за прозрачност при представителство на интереси: the same dropped § 1, but amended in ДВ бр. 55/2026, so the investigation reads whether that amendment touched § 1; if it did not, § 1 is filled from the promulgated text, and if it did, the act stops with a case file. Указ № 883: today from strategy.bg; its route is a source before the archive (S4).

Done when the list is committed with its tests, every entry names its route, and a write of the transport law from lex.bg is refused by name. and the transport law is committed with § 1 and its twelve definitions.

S2

What lex.bg gets wrong

  • Run the dropped-section detector (FR-042) over every raw page, and check its 103 candidates against the Gazette.
  • For a sample of acts in each profile from S3, compare lex.bg with the Gazette and name each kind of content error found, as distinct from formatting.
  • Each kind gets its evidence and its detector where the form allows one.

Done when a register of lex.bg's error kinds is committed, and every act confirmed wrong is committed correct from its route or stopped with a case file. Most such acts have been amended, so most need plan step 5's history route.

S3

A profile of every act

  • Per act: its kind, its Gazette era (HTML from 2003, PDF 1989–2002, before 1989), its grade, its layout features (tables, annexes, images, unnumbered paragraphs), its known defects and its source.
  • Counted by script from the raw pages where the feature has a fixed form; judged by a model run where it does not.
  • For the 107 acts begun before the archive, whether each is still in force, checked against the Gazette's 202 repeal titles not yet attributed to an act.

Done when the register covers all 3,624 acts, with the verified list of acts in force from before 1989.

S4

Sources for the acts before the archive

  • For each act on S3's list, which source counts as authoritative: the Ministry of Justice portal, the Gazette's issue PDFs from 1989, strategy.bg or another state source.
  • Trace where the full text of Указ № 883 was found, and look for ДВ бр. 39/1974.
  • A short APIS subscription is a spending decision for the owner, with its terms read against D-039.

Done when every act on S3's list is committed from its named source, or from lex.bg once checked against it.

S5

The audit, by profile

  • The remaining 3,574 acts, in batches of one profile each, each batch agreed with the owner.
  • A new form becomes a ruling and its test; an act with lost text goes on the list and into its route.

Done when every act is committed correct through the gate, from lex.bg or from its route, or stopped with a case file the owner has read. That is the starting state; its quality bar is the owner’s to set.

Task 2: the forward rebuild

F1 · plan step 5

The engine: built, verified per kind of act, then used case by case

  • Build: split each material, attribute it to the act it changes, turn its instructions into changes, apply them and check the result; a new act is read whole through task 1's path.
  • Verify: for a few representative acts of each kind, chosen from S3's profiles and agreed with the owner, rebuild the complete history from the Gazette and compare the result with lex.bg and the Ministry of Justice portal, settling every difference. This is the evidence of whether the engine can be built.
  • Use: on each new issue (F2), and for an act's complete history only case by case, where one is needed: a difference to settle, a gap in a lex.bg fetch to check.
  • Bounded like every route: what it cannot apply with certainty stops with a case file.

Done when the representative acts of every kind are committed with their history rebuilt from the Gazette, every difference from the witnesses settled and the engine's errors counted per kind; acts or kinds it cannot do stop with case files the owner reads.

F2 · plan step 8

The daily loop

A declared scheduled loop: the new Gazette issues since the last ledger mark, through the engine, one pull request per run. Supervised first, then active.

Done when the loop has kept the corpus current for a period the owner sets, with its case files read.

F3 · my addition

The periodic test against lex.bg

  • On a schedule the owner sets, every act the engine changed is compared with lex.bg and with the Ministry of Justice portal.
  • Each difference is settled against the Gazette as the engine's error, the witness's error or a timing difference, and recorded as data.
  • The count of the engine's errors per period is the quality measure; an engine error is fixed in the engine and the act re-committed.

Done when the first test is committed with its settled differences and its measure.
